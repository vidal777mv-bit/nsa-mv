    How we know

    The method, the two validation tests and the mission controls. Neither validation test passed. Their numbers are shown as they came out.

    1. 1 · Measure

      Six measures are computed on each 8 km circle from elevation models, on a 100 m grid: steepness, bumpiness across scales, bowl vs plateau, grain direction, ridges and hollows. The 3D view draws the same terrain on a 30 m grid.

    2. 2 · Compare

      Each twin gets a distance D: the root mean square of the differences on the three scored measures (steepness, bowl vs plateau, bumpiness across scales), each divided by its sigma. Grain direction, ridges and hollows are context and do not change D. Twins closer than the sensor noise are tied, and the list says so.

    3. 3 · Check

      The method was tested on whether it separates terrain types (impact craters, volcanic fields, dune fields, canyons, plains). Version 1.2 was tested on 20 regions. Version 1.3 was designed after looking at the 1.2 results, so it was tested on 15 new regions, measured once.

    Validation

    Mission controls

    The landing limit must cover at least 50% of each of six reference sites with completed landings. Values come from their comparison cards.

    Limits of this atlas

    • A close match in the measures does not mean the place is safe or the same. It means these measures agree.
    • The 3D view uses a 30 m grid; the measures use 100 m. A 100 m slope is gentler than the slope a lander sees at its own scale.
    • The twin is oriented for display only. Rotation does not change the score.
    • The search pool is mostly Sahara, Arabia, Central Asia and, since the Arctic was added, Greenland, Svalbard, Arctic Canada and Siberia. Most mission limits have no stated baseline. Hurst (bumpiness across scales) is comparative.
    • Earth cannot reproduce reduced gravity, vacuum or a thin atmosphere, or radiation.
